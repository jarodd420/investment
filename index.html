How the figure is made

Implied upside is the average analyst price target minus the last regular-session close, divided by that close. Every public name uses that figure, and a higher number ranks first. A company that is not publicly traded stays on the list and is marked not rated, with no ticker, price, or score invented for it.

SpaceX was checked against its June 2026 Nasdaq listing. It trades as SPCX, so it is rated with the same figure as Apple, Amazon, NVIDIA, and Microsoft.

The names under consideration use that same figure and are not holdings. Google is scored as Alphabet Class A, ticker GOOGL. Class C, ticker GOOG, was checked and is not the share used here. Broadcom, Meta, and Eli Lilly were suggested for the seat they could fill. That suggestion is why they are on the list. Their order is still the upside figure. The Broadcom note beside that figure is a separate write-up of the company’s reported results.

Under each name, a conservative book comments on the same close and the same average target, and names the weight that gap can bear. A wide gap, or a listing with only a few public months, takes a small seat. A modest gap on a franchise the company already operates takes an ordinary or full weight. A close above the average target is a hold. Those weights leave the upside order unchanged.

Closes are the October 16, 2026, 4:00 p.m. ET regular session. Average targets were retrieved October 17, 2026. These numbers are a dated snapshot, not a live quote, and they are not investment advice.
